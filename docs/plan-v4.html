Pitch

Hailprint pulls NOAA's MRMS hail-size radar product every night, draws the hail swath over every parcel in Harris County, and then grades its own map against NOAA storm reports. Every Texas storm adds a swath, a comparison, and a score, with no manual input. The output is a live map, a per-storm exposure report, and a six-season verification table that no commercial vendor publishes.

Demo · 0:00Pick a storm date. The swath lands on the county in size classes from 0.75 to 2.5+ inch.
Demo · 0:40Zoom to a ZIP. Each parcel carries its radar-estimated hail size and a confidence band.
Demo · 1:20Open the scorecard. Hits, misses, false alarms against NOAA reports for that storm, and the six-season average.

Why a chat LLM cannot replace it. The product is a running pipeline (GRIB2 decoding, raster-to-vector, spatial joins over more than a million parcels, nightly scheduling) plus a scorecard that only exists after seasons of operation. Claude can write the code. It cannot be the system, and it cannot have run since 2020.

2

Why it stands out

Nobody open has done it

GitHub holds three zero-star hail-map repos (one pushed 2026-10-24). None joins to parcels and none publishes verification scores. Incumbents (Interactive Hail Maps, HailTrace, HailStrike, CoreLogic, Verisk) all start from the same free NEXRAD feed and sell meteorologist review on top.

The money is loud and local

State Farm alone paid more than $5.6B in hail claims in 2025, $1.4B of it in Texas. CoreLogic counts 2.13M Houston homes at moderate-or-higher hail risk. Texas has been the hail-damage capital for 11 straight years.

Honesty is the moat

The published skill ceiling for radar hail estimates is low: Heidke skill score 0.38 at best. Hailprint leads with that number and shows per-storm precision instead of claiming accuracy. Every vendor hides it. A hiring manager reads judgment.

Five skills in one repo

Data engineering (S3, GRIB2, nightly batch), geospatial computation (contours, joins, tiles), statistics (POD, FAR, CSI, HSS, preliminary vs final truth), product sense with a dollar figure, and a shipped front end.

Scorecard design

Ground truth, two columns

SPC preliminary reports (yesterday_hail.csv, hundredths of an inch, 12Z to 12Z day) the next morning. NCEI Storm Events when the month closes, 75 to 90 days later. The scorecard shows both, so the preliminary-vs-final drift is visible instead of hidden.

Matching rule

A report counts as a hit when a swath pixel at or above the threshold lies within a neighborhood radius (default 5 km) on the storm day. Swath area with no report nearby counts as a false alarm only where report density is adequate. Sparse areas get an unverifiable class, following the neighborhood method in Ortega 2018.

Metrics

Per storm and per season: probability of detection, false alarm ratio, critical success index, Heidke skill score, at 25.4 mm and 30 mm. Plus swath area in square kilometers and parcel count per size class.

Acceptance

The six-season table reproduces the literature ceiling: HSS between 0.25 and 0.45 at 30 mm against SPC reports. A result far above the literature means a matching bug, not a breakthrough. The plan treats it that way.

Architecture

Pull

Anonymous S3 from noaa-mrms-pds; SPC CSV; NCEI monthly CSV; HCAD quarterly.

Decode, crop

cfgrib + xarray; county bounding box; per-day raster.

Swaths

rasterio.features + shapely; seven size classes; GeoParquet.

Parcel join

DuckDB spatial or GeoPandas STRtree; owner fields dropped at load.

Verify

Neighborhood matching; POD / FAR / CSI / HSS; scorecard.json.

Tiles, map

tippecanoe to PMTiles on R2; MapLibre GL; no tile server.

Pipeline: Python

GRIB2 tooling (cfgrib, wgrib2, xarray, rasterio) is mature in Python and poor in JavaScript. One package, one scheduler entry point, typed with pydantic for the config.

Front end: TypeScript

MapLibre GL JS with the pmtiles protocol on a static site. Storm-date picker, two layers, scorecard panel. Panel and report reuse the tokens from your design-system repo.

Storage

Cropped daily rasters are a few megabytes. The full backtest is under 2 GB of source files and a few hundred megabytes of outputs. Tiles stay under 200 MB.

Privacy rule

HCAD parcel attributes include owner names and mailing addresses. The loader drops them before anything is written for publishing. The public map carries parcel ID, geometry, land-use class, year built, and improvement area only. No homeowner contact data, ever, including as a paid add-on.

Legal wording rule

Outputs say "radar-estimated hail size at this parcel" with a confidence band. The word "damaged" never appears. Texas has a hostile hail-claim litigation history (SB 1060 and SB 1628, 2015 to 2017; a 2017 roofer class action over hail solicitation). The map is an exposure estimate, not a damage finding.
